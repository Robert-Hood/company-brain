# Objection handling

## "We already have an offshore team doing this"

Do not attack the team. Ask two questions: what percentage of verifications come back needing
re-work, and how long the queue is on a Monday morning. Most groups do not know the first number and
wince at the second. The pitch is not "replace your team", it is "your team stops doing the 70% that
is mechanical and starts doing the 30% that needs a phone call".

## "How is this different from the AI tools we keep getting pitched?"

Every one of those returns a payer response and calls it done. We put a human on every exception
before it reaches your front desk. Ask them what happens today when the portal returns something
ambiguous. That is the whole difference and it is worth a full minute.

## "$1.75 is more than we pay now"

You pay per attempt, we charge per completed verification. Ask for their attempt to completion
ratio. If they run 1.4 attempts per completed verification at $1.30, they are at $1.82 and they have
a queue.

## "We had a bad integration experience"

Almost always Dentrix or a custom middleware project. Acknowledge it, do not argue. Offer a
read-only pilot: we pull the schedule, we return verifications, nothing writes back to their system
until they have seen 30 days of output they trust. Bring Tom Beckett to the second call.

## "Can you do claims too?"

Not today. Say that clearly. Then ask what happens when a claim comes back denied and whether they
can trace it to the eligibility check. Nobody can. That is the roadmap conversation and it is a
better one than pretending we do claims.

## "We need to talk to our PMS vendor first"

Fine, but find out whether that is a real dependency or a delay tactic. Open Dental and Carestack
are permissive. Eaglesoft is slower. Dentrix Ascend needs a partner agreement that takes weeks, so
if they are on Dentrix Ascend, start that paperwork on the first call.
